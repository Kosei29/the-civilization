# Historical Timeline

## Year 1

### Day 1 — The Beginning

The civilization's historical record begins.

No established political boundaries, institutions, major settlements, leaders, wars, discoveries, or disasters have yet been recorded.

This is the baseline from which all future history will be measured.

### Days 2–22 — The First Cultivation

The population began deliberately transforming the land surrounding the founding settlement. Workers cleared vegetation, prepared soil, and established approximately 0.6 km² of agricultural plots. Sixty-five people are now primarily assigned to farming, with the first organized fields ready for planting by Day 21.

At the same time, construction crews began replacing temporary shelter with basic permanent dwellings. By Day 20, twenty-four simple dwellings had been completed around the founding settlement. The community is beginning to resemble a permanent village rather than a temporary camp.

The work consumed significant quantities of wood, stone, and treasury resources. No formal government or territorial survey has yet been established, and the wider territory remains largely unknown.

**Result:** The civilization has secured the beginnings of a food-producing settlement and permanent housing, but its agricultural output has not yet matured enough to remove the need for careful resource management.

### Days 23–45 — Livestock and Household Food Security

The village began building household-adjacent livestock pens. Hunting parties searched for animals suitable for both meat and breeding, and the first managed breeding stock was brought into the settlement.

By Day 45, the community had begun shifting from purely agricultural subsistence toward a mixed farming-and-livestock system. The purpose was not simply to increase food, but to reduce the amount of time households needed to spend continuously searching for food, leaving more time for family and other communal work.

### Days 46–60 — First Organized Exploration

As agriculture and livestock routines stabilized, five members of the militia were assigned to exploration. Their mission was to survey the surrounding land, identify water and useful resources, observe wildlife and potential dangers, and look for signs of other people.

By Day 60, approximately 2.1 km² had been surveyed. The wider territory remained largely unknown.

### Days 61–72 — The Village Builds Its First Defense

With half of the military away, thirty villagers were temporarily assigned to construct a log fence around the settlement's most densely occupied core. The work was intended to create a defensible center without treating the fence as a territorial border.

By Day 72, the fence was approximately 80% complete. Only five defenders remained immediately available at the village while the exploration party was away.

### Days 73–88 — Production Resumes and Roads Appear

The village returned sixty farmers to full agricultural work. Cultivation expanded to approximately 0.8 km², the first harvest began producing stored food, and households began propagating useful fruit plants and other edible plants in suitable lawn areas.

Workers established simple dirt paths connecting the main residential area, fields, livestock areas, and household clusters. These paths created the village's first recognizable internal road network.

### Days 89–105 — The Search for the Explorers

Normal production continued while the five explorers remained away. Livestock breeding continued, household gardens developed, and the village population began to grow naturally.

Two workers were then sent to follow the explorers' known route and determine where the expedition had gone. Their purpose was not to replace the exploration mission, but to restore reliable contact with the missing party.

During the search, the two workers discovered signs of the explorers near a freshwater stream north of the previously surveyed area. On Day 105 they established visual contact with all five explorers and confirmed that the entire expedition was alive and continuing its work.

The northern freshwater stream is now the first newly reported geographic feature outside the current official survey. Its surrounding area has not yet been fully mapped.

Two births were also recorded during this period, bringing the population from 100 to 102. This establishes an important principle of the civilization's demographic history: population is allowed to grow naturally through births, alongside deaths, migration, and other demographic changes.

**Result:** The civilization now has 102 people, a functioning agricultural and livestock base, a defended village core, basic internal roads, and an active exploration party operating beyond the mapped territory. The village has also confirmed its first significant freshwater feature beyond the current survey zone.
